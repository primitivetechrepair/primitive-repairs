Displays, Controllers and Readers

Screen, touch, Joy-Con, game-card, and microSD problems.

The display panel, touch layer, controller, console rail, analog stick, button flex, game-card reader, headphone socket, and microSD reader are separate components. The symptom and affected model determine which assembly requires testing.

LCD or OLED Failure

Black areas, colored lines, flickering, image loss, pressure damage, discoloration, dead pixels, or a display that remains black while the console produces sound.

Touch-Layer Problems

The display image remains visible but taps, swipes, or specific areas do not respond, register incorrectly, or activate without input.

Joy-Con Drift and Analog Sticks

Characters or menus move without input, sticks do not return to center, movement is inconsistent, or calibration does not correct the behavior.

Joy-Con Rail and Connection Faults

Controllers are not detected when attached, disconnect during play, fail to charge, feel loose, or have damaged console-side or controller-side rail contacts.

Game-Card Reader Problems

Physical games are not detected, produce read errors, require repeated insertion, or stopped working after cartridge, impact, or liquid damage.

microSD Reader Problems

Storage cards disappear, produce errors, are not detected, work intermittently, or have damaged reader contacts, sockets, or board connections.

Fan and Cooling Problems

Heat and Shutdowns

Repeated thermal warnings, shutdowns under load, abnormal heat near the charging area, battery heat, or a system that becomes hot before completing startup.

Liquid Exposure and Corrosion

Charging faults, display problems, controller connection issues, corrosion, short circuits, reader failure, sound problems, and no power after liquid contact.

Swollen Battery Safety

Stop charging when safe and do not compress a swollen housing, raised screen, or separated rear panel. Expansion can damage the display, frame, and internal components.

Board-Level Repair

When Nintendo Switch repair requires microsoldering.

Board-level work may be required when a connector is soldered directly to the motherboard, copper pads or traces are torn, charging or video circuitry has failed, components are missing, corrosion has damaged circuits, or a previous repair attempt caused additional damage.

USB-C Port Replacement

Precision removal and installation of board-mounted USB-C connectors, followed by inspection of all contacts, anchors, pads, and nearby components.

Lifted Pad and Trace Repair

Missing connector pads, torn signal paths, damaged anchors, broken vias, and incomplete board connections may require reconstruction or jumper work.

Charging and Power Controllers

Failed charging, power-delivery, battery-management, or supporting circuits may cause no charge, unstable power, excess current draw, or complete no power.

Dock and Video Signal Circuits

Damaged filters, USB-C signal lines, missing components, video-output circuitry, or board faults may prevent dock communication and television output.

Corrosion and Short-Circuit Repair

Liquid residue, missing copper, damaged components, shorted rails, and corrosion beneath connectors may require cleaning, testing, and circuit repair.

Previous Repair Damage

Excessive heat, solder bridges, incorrect parts, missing components, damaged board layers, loose jumpers, and incomplete port work can increase repair complexity.

Which Nintendo Switch models do you repair?

Do you repair Nintendo Switch USB-C charging ports?

Yes, on supported systems. We inspect the connector, internal contacts, anchors, solder pads, traces, charging behavior, dock communication, and nearby board components before confirming replacement.

Why does my Switch charge but not display on the television?

Possible causes include the dock, power adapter, HDMI cable, display input, USB-C connector, signal lines, filters, video-output circuitry, or another board-level fault.

Do you replace Switch LCD, OLED, and touch screens?

Yes, on supported models. LCD or OLED image failure and touchscreen failure are separate problems, so we review the visible image, touch response, frame, impact damage, and power behavior first.

Do you repair Joy-Con drift and controller rails?

We review analog-stick drift, button problems, charging failure, controller detection, loose attachment, damaged rail contacts, and console-side or Joy-Con-side connection faults.

Do you repair game-card and microSD readers?

Yes, on supported systems. Reader problems may involve the cartridge or storage card, damaged contacts, reader sockets, internal flex cables, connectors, liquid exposure, or board-level damage.

Can liquid-damaged Nintendo Switch systems be repaired?

We review supported systems for corrosion, charging faults, display problems, controller-rail failure, reader problems, sound issues, short circuits, and no-power symptoms. Repair options depend on the extent and location of the damage.

Is pricing confirmed before Nintendo Switch repair begins?

Yes. Final pricing depends on the exact model, failed component, required parts, physical or liquid damage, prior repair work, board-level reconstruction, and inspection findings. Pricing is provided for approval before paid work begins.

Do Nintendo Switch repairs include a warranty?
